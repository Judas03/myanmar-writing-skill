# Matching a Burmese Brand Voice

Use approved examples to learn how this writer makes choices. Do not imitate
surface quirks before understanding the pattern.

## Useful source data

Ten to twenty approved posts from the same writer or brand can establish a
workable profile. More examples help when they cover the three target formats.
The most useful set includes:

- final published copy, not early drafts;
- the platform and purpose of each piece;
- the intended audience;
- examples the user considers especially successful or unsuccessful;
- any non-negotiable spellings, banned claims, or required calls to action.

Engagement numbers can be context, but do not assume the wording caused the
performance. Topic, timing, media, promotion, and audience size also matter.

## Derive the profile

Look for repeated evidence in these dimensions:

| Dimension | Observe |
|---|---|
| Relationship | peer, teacher, expert, institution, friend, seller |
| Politeness | sentence endings, requests, honorifics, softeners |
| Point of view | omitted subject, brand `we`, named brand, gendered self-reference |
| Vocabulary | Burmese-English balance, preferred spellings, specialist terms |
| Rhythm | sentence length, paragraph length, questions, fragments |
| Openings | direct news, problem, story, question, claim |
| Structure | narrative, sections, bullets, detail blocks |
| Decoration | emoji, dividers, hashtags, bold Unicode characters |
| Persuasion | proof, specificity, urgency, reassurance, CTA style |
| Boundaries | words, claims, jokes, and tones the brand avoids |

Separate stable voice from format behavior. A blog and a Facebook post from the
same writer should share vocabulary and attitude without sharing the same
layout.

## Weight evidence carefully

1. Follow explicit user instructions.
2. Follow approved examples from the same brand and format.
3. Follow approved examples from the same brand in another format.
4. Use the general guidance in this skill only where the examples are silent.

Do not learn spelling mistakes, encoding errors, factual mistakes, accidental
repetition, copied quotations, or one-off campaign gimmicks as brand voice. If
examples conflict, prefer the most recent approved pattern or briefly ask which
direction to follow when the difference would be obvious to readers.

## Apply without impersonating mechanically

Match tendencies, not exact phrases. Avoid lifting a memorable hook, anecdote,
or sentence from a sample into a new post. Preserve the new brief's meaning and
let the profile guide word choice, rhythm, and presentation.

Before delivery, compare the draft with two or three closest examples. Check
whether the opening, endings, code-switching, paragraph rhythm, and CTA would
feel at home beside them.
