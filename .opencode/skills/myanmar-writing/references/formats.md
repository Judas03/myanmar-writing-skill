# Platform Formats

Choose structure from the communication goal. These are patterns, not forms
that every piece must fill.

## Facebook posts

Facebook copy is read quickly on a phone. Give the first one or two lines a
clear job: surface a useful fact, a recognizable problem, a concrete result,
or genuine news. Do not use fake curiosity or hide the subject merely to force
a click.

A useful order for promotional or informational posts is:

1. relevant opening;
2. why it matters to this audience;
3. the useful details or proof;
4. next step, only when there is one.

Keep paragraphs short enough to scan. Use bullets when readers need to compare
features, dates, requirements, or steps. Do not turn ordinary prose into a wall
of one-line fragments.

Emoji can mark sections or mood when the brand uses them. A professional post
may need none. Hashtags should be relevant and searchable; omit them when they
add no discovery value. Never add a phone number, link, price, deadline, or
scarcity claim that was not supplied.

For announcements, make the offer, audience, date, location, price, and action
easy to find. For thought-leadership posts, prioritize the idea and evidence
over a sales call to action.

## Website copy

First identify the page type: home, landing, about, service, product, FAQ, or
article page. Each section should answer a reader question.

Typical landing or service flow:

- a clear headline naming the value or outcome;
- a supporting line that adds audience, mechanism, or distinction;
- specific benefits or capabilities;
- evidence, process, or reassurance;
- a precise next action.

A benefit section may explain why a supplied capability matters, but it must
not invent how the product works. Do not assume dashboards, calendars,
notifications, automation, real-time sync, reports, exports, reminders,
permissions, trials, or instant setup unless the brief states them. Do not turn
`customer history` into particular stored fields or promise that staff will
never need to ask a customer again.

When the user requests the same number of sections as supplied capabilities,
map them one-to-one. A sparse safe pattern is:

- heading: the supplied capability in reader language;
- body: where or how the brief explicitly says it can be managed;
- no imagined example, pain scenario, UI behavior, or guaranteed result.

For example, `manage bookings from a phone` supports `Booking တွေကို ဖုန်းကနေ
စီမံနိုင်ပါတယ်။` It does not support claims that the screen opens instantly,
shows today's arrivals, prevents forgetting, replaces paper, or assigns a
booking to a particular staff member. `Manage from a phone` also does not by
itself prove that a computer is unnecessary.

Use the audience's domain term accurately. Making copy non-technical means
explaining clearly, not replacing a term with a different business concept.
For salons, `appointment`, `booking`, or `ရက်ချိန်း` is not the same as an
order (`မှာယူမှု`). Follow supplied brand language when available.

Use headings that remain meaningful when skimmed alone. Prefer short sentences
for UI labels, buttons, navigation, forms, errors, and helper text. Keep the
same term for the same action across the page.

Do not include drafting labels such as `Hero`, `H1`, `Sub`, `Body`, or
`Headline` in paste-ready copy unless the user requests a content-spec handoff.
Present actual headings and body copy. If the requested CTA channel or product
availability is unknown, use a neutral action such as learning more; do not
promise immediate access, a free trial, registration, or contact method.
`ပိုမိုလေ့လာရန်` is neutral. `စမ်းကြည့်ပါ`, `စတင်သုံးပါ`, and
`ဆက်သွယ်မေးမြန်းပါ` each assert an available path and require support.

Search keywords may appear in titles, introductions, and relevant headings,
but never repeat them at the cost of natural Burmese. Do not create performance,
security, customer, or ranking claims without evidence.

If the user needs markup, keep content and markup separate enough to edit. Use
`lang="my"`, a Myanmar-capable font, comfortable line height, and wrapping that
does not split Myanmar glyph clusters. These are implementation notes, not text
to place in the copy.

## Blog posts

Choose a title that promises the article's real subject, not a broader or more
sensational one. The opening should establish the reader's question, the
article's angle, or the practical stakes without a generic history lesson.

Organize the article around the reader's reasoning:

1. establish the problem or claim;
2. explain the key ideas in a useful order;
3. support them with supplied examples, evidence, or steps;
4. address an important limitation or common misunderstanding;
5. close by resolving the opening question or giving the appropriate next
   step.

Use headings when the topic changes, not after an arbitrary number of
paragraphs. Transitions should carry the logic; avoid repeating the article
title in different words at the start of every section.

For tutorials, preserve commands and UI labels exactly and distinguish action
from explanation. For analysis, separate fact, interpretation, and opinion.
For sourced posts, never fabricate citations or imply that an unsourced claim
was verified.

## Length

Follow the user's requested limit or the publishing field's limit. Otherwise,
write only as long as the idea needs. A complete short post is better than a
padded long one; a useful blog should not be cut merely to satisfy an invented
word count.
