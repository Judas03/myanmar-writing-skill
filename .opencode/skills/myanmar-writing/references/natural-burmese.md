# Natural Burmese

Use this guide to remove translated, ceremonial, or generic-AI phrasing. It is
a decision guide, not a list of mandatory substitutions.

Its general language foundation is consistent with Microsoft's Burmese
localization guidance: clear, friendly, concise language; short readable
sentences; active voice; everyday wording; intent-based rather than literal
translation; verb-final clauses; and omission of obvious personal pronouns.
That source is written for product localization, so its UI-specific choices are
not treated as universal rules for Facebook or editorial writing.

## Pick a spoken-written level

Natural Burmese changes with the relationship between writer and reader.

- **Conversational:** Facebook communities, creator posts, familiar brands.
  Shorter paragraphs and everyday endings such as `တယ်`, `ပါတယ်`, `မယ်`,
  `နော်` when the brand actually uses them.
- **Polished:** most businesses, services, education, and general websites.
  Warm and clear without sounding like speech transcribed word for word.
  `ပါတယ်` is common, but should not end every sentence.
- **Formal:** official statements, policies, institutional reports, or an
  explicitly formal brand. Literary endings such as `သည်` can fit here, but a
  whole document of repeated `ဖြစ်သည်` still sounds mechanical.

Do not assume Facebook must be casual or a website must be formal. Audience and
brand evidence decide.

## Build Burmese clauses, not translated English

Burmese is normally verb-final and often leaves an obvious subject unstated.
Translate the idea, then rebuild the clause.

- Replace English noun stacks with a short clause or two.
- Prefer an active actor when the actor matters. Omit it when context makes it
  obvious.
- Put the reason, condition, time, or context before the main result when that
  is how a speaker would tell the thought.
- Split a long English sentence when its clauses compete for attention.
- Combine choppy source sentences when Burmese particles can carry the
  relationship naturally.

Avoid mirroring every `we`, `you`, `it`, and `this`. Repeated `ကျွန်ုပ်တို့`,
`သင်`, `၎င်း`, and `ဤအရာ` usually signal literal translation. Use a noun once,
then omit the subject where it remains clear.

## Keep the rhythm alive

Do not manufacture variety by swapping synonyms at random. Vary the shape of
the thought:

- a short direct sentence after a longer explanation;
- a question only when it is a real reader question;
- a list when the items are genuinely parallel;
- a concrete example instead of another abstract claim;
- a paragraph break when the reader's focus changes.

Watch for repeated endings such as `ဖြစ်ပါတယ်`, `ရှိပါတယ်`, `ရရှိနိုင်ပါတယ်`,
and `ပြုလုပ်နိုင်ပါတယ်`. Some may be correct. Rewrite only the repetitive or
indirect ones, using the actual verb whenever possible.

## Prefer concrete language

Generic claims sound translated even when their grammar is correct.

- Replace `အကောင်းဆုံးအတွေ့အကြုံကို ပေးစွမ်းနိုင်ရန်` with what the reader can
  actually do or receive.
- Replace `ထိရောက်မှုတိုးတက်စေပါသည်` with the observable improvement.
- Do not open with `ယနေ့ခေတ်တွင်...` unless the time contrast is the point.
- Do not add `အားလုံးပဲ မင်္ဂလာပါ`, `သတင်းကောင်းပါးချင်ပါတယ်`, or exaggerated
  excitement unless it belongs to the writer's real voice.

Specifics such as who, what, when, where, how, and why create trust. Never
invent those specifics to make copy stronger.

## Politeness and point of view

- Preserve the writer's known self-reference. If gender is unknown, do not
  choose `ကျွန်တော်` or `ကျွန်မ`.
- Use honorifics and kinship terms only when the relationship supports them.
- Avoid commanding readers when a polite invitation is more appropriate.
- Do not force `ရှင်`, `ခင်ဗျာ`, `နော်`, or `ဗျာ` into a brand voice that has
  not established them.
- Use `မိတ်ဆွေ`, `လူကြီးမင်း`, and `သင်` sparingly. They create a strong and
  sometimes unwanted tone.

## Punctuation and spacing

Use Unicode Burmese punctuation and line breaks consistently with the chosen
platform. `။` and `၊` are dependable for Burmese prose; `?` and `!` can be
natural in contemporary social copy when used lightly.

In formal prose, questions commonly end with `။`; in conversational digital
copy, `?` can be used when context and brand style support it. Do not mix styles
randomly within one piece.

Burmese does not separate every word with a space. Use spaces to clarify
phrases and script changes, but never split a syllable cluster or a bound
particle from the word it belongs to. Around English terms, favour readable
forms such as `AI ကို`, `Facebook မှာ`, and `content ရေးတဲ့အခါ`. Preserve an
established brand style when it is clear and readable.

For digital content, Arabic digits are usually easiest to scan. Burmese digits
can fit literary prose, a traditional brand style, or supplied copy. Preserve
phone numbers, prices, dates, measurements, model numbers, and URLs exactly
unless conversion is requested.

## Localizing from English

1. Extract the factual meaning, intent, audience, and emotional temperature.
2. Mark names, numbers, links, legal wording, and product claims that must not
   drift.
3. Draft the paragraph naturally in Burmese without looking for a one-to-one
   match for every source word.
4. Restore necessary English terms in the form the audience recognizes.
5. Compare source and draft for omissions, additions, certainty changes, and
   altered calls to action.

Do not explain English idioms literally. Replace them with the intended meaning
or a Burmese expression that fits the same situation. Do not import cultural
references that the audience would need explained unless the reference itself
matters.

## Editing test

Read the draft as spoken Burmese. If a phrase is grammatically possible but not
something the intended writer would naturally say, rewrite the whole clause.
Do not keep polishing a faulty word-for-word structure.
